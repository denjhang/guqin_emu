Artificial Intelligence

AI Interpretation for Guqin Scores (AI 釋譜) · Candle · String Theory, Soaring Dragon

With Wei Bing, Youdi Wang co-directs AI Shi Pu — AI Interpretation for Guqin Scores (AI 釋譜), an AI-based platform for digitizing guqin tablature (jianzipu), developed with the Department of Music AI and Music Information Technology at the Central Conservatory of Music. Wei Bing leads the technology; she serves as Artistic Director for the guqin. The first task was to digitize the scores and build a dataset of historical guqin tablature from scratch. The project created the world's first AI-generated Guqin piece, Candle — every note generated by AI, then arranged and notated by Youdi Wang — premiered at her doctoral graduation concert on 24 December 2021 (Central Conservatory · Beijing Daily).

Carrying the dataset and the generative model one step further comes String Theory, Soaring Dragon — written for guqin, electronic music and AI. Composed by Liu Enyang; visuals, algorithms and interaction design by Wei Bing; guqin by Youdi Wang. The title comes from string theory in physics: elementary particles are one-dimensional "strings" whose vibration modes correspond to different particles — and the guqin is among the oldest stringed instruments of China, inscribed on the UNESCO intangible heritage list in 2003. Set against the traditional piece Soaring Dragon, the work trains a deep-learning model on the team's own dataset of traditional guqin repertoire to generate new guqin music, then combines it with electronic music and high-precision motion capture, so that a human performer, virtual instruments and AI-generated sound meet in one audiovisual space.

The Guqin Art Week in the Greater Bay Area

Funded by the China National Arts Fund (國家藝術基金) in 2024 · also funded as a cultural exchange project by the Culture, Sports and Tourism Bureau, HKSAR

On 17 January 2025 the inaugural Guqin Art Week in the Greater Bay Area (大灣區古琴藝術週), funded by the China National Arts Fund in 2024, opened at the Huaqiang Culture and Arts Centre in Guangming District, Shenzhen. It was reported as the largest guqin cultural exchange event held in the Guangdong–Hong Kong–Macao Greater Bay Area in recent years, combining online and on-site programmes: guqin concerts, art showcases, a new-works concert, academic forums, symposia and an art-and-technology exhibition.

Wu Wenguang, senior professor at the China Conservatory of Music and a towering figure in the guqin world, served as Honorary Chair; Zhao Jiazhen, professor and doctoral supervisor at the Central Conservatory of Music, served as Honorary Chair and Chair of the jury. Youdi Wang and Wei Bing, associate professor in the Department of Music AI and Music Information Technology at the Central Conservatory, are its co-founders; she serves as Executive Chair and Wei Bing as technical director.

At the opening she described the aim: to build an "Guqin Arts Plus" cultural calling card rooted in the Greater Bay Area, reaching the whole country and facing the world, and to establish a platform integrating industry, education, research, exhibition and outreach. Three themes shaped the first edition — masters gathered, players in dialogue, and new works assembled. "I am deeply grateful to the National Arts Fund and the Hong Kong SAR Government for their recognition, trust and funding, which allowed this project to grow from a seed and be carried through."

Capriccio on the Town of Wei

Composed by Youdi Wang　2019　·　guqin and dizi

Composed by Youdi Wang in 2019, Capriccio on the Town of Wei takes its inspiration from Song of Weicheng by the Tang poet Wang Wei, also known as Seeing Yuan Er Off on a Mission to Anxi: "Morning rain in Weicheng dampens the light dust; the inn stands green, the willows freshly new. Let me urge on you one more cup of wine — west of Yang Pass there will be no old friends." Through the northwestern frontier landscape and a cup raised with an old friend, the poem carries a deep and unhurried grief at parting; its lines read like a painting, and it has remained famous for a thousand years. Later musicians drew on the same material for the guqin classic Three Variations on Yang Pass, whose threefold repetition returns again and again to the parting; since the Ming dynasty it has been preserved in many tablature collections.

Capriccio on the Town of Wei extends from that tradition. It opens with the clear, round timbre of the guqin against the distant, floating line of the dizi, drawing the scene: lone smoke over the desert, the sun setting on the long river, old friends drinking, reluctant to part. In the fast section guqin and dizi turn lively and quick, entering into dialogue — as though setting out west from Weicheng and passing through the customs and colours of the Western Regions. The ending returns to the long line of the opening, scene and feeling merged, the aftertaste unbroken.

Or as it may be put: a rhapsody from Weicheng, begun in the high Tang. Wang Wei then, his feeling turned one way. Cups raised together, the desert bare. Three variations chanted, Yang Pass the road home.

Discovering with Looking Back: the Qin

Wei Bing　2023　·　the only invited artwork to attend the 2023 Zhongguancun Digital Music Festival

A multimedia interactive installation. Working through audiovisual synaesthesia, it takes the viewer's movement as the object of interaction and answers with corresponding guqin sound, staging an abstract and unfixed dialogue between the work and the person in front of it — an invitation to think about art and technology, technology and the humanities, and where the boundary between human and artificial thought may lie.

Interaction Between Qin and Human No.1

Wei Bing　2021–2023　·　guqin recorded by Youdi Wang

Using AI image-recognition algorithms, the work invites the audience into the act of turning jianzipu tablature into guqin music. A participant may follow the guidance or simply follow their own ear, trying, reading and trying again — and hearing how a different interaction yields a different piece.
